For a Quiet Dread night — dread on foot, in daylight, nothing gory or torture-heavy — here's where I'd go, in the spirit of what you loved in *The Vanishing* and liked in *Zodiac*.

**I'd start with *Memories of Murder* (2003).** Bong Joon-ho's investigation into a string of rural killings plays out almost entirely in broad daylight, in open fields and paddy roads — the dread comes from procedure and futility, not from anything shown. It's exactly the "kept a step behind, never shouted at" feeling, stretched over real patience.

From there:

- **If you want to stay close to Zodiac's obsessive-investigator register** — *Insomnia* (2002). Nolan's Alaska is lit almost too bright to hide in, and the dread is guilt eating a good detective in daylight that never lets up.
- **If you want the dread more political and composed** — *The Ghost Writer* (2010). Polanski at his most controlled: a ghostwriter slowly realizing the danger he's in, all quiet rooms and grey coastal light, nothing violent at all.
- **If you're willing to drift further, into moral rot rather than crime** — *A Simple Plan* (1998). Snow-bright daylight, a plan curdling in slow motion, dread that comes entirely from watching ordinary people decide to become something else.
